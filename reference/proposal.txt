PROJECT PROPOSAL
DESERT VOICE PROJECT 


Rajasthani Folk Artists Documentation,
Promotion and Booking Facilitation Initiative


An initiative by the young minds of
Rajasthan Leaders Association (RLA)


1. Executive Summary
The Desert Voice Project is a practical initiative of Rajasthan Leaders Association (RLA) to build a reliable bridge between Rajasthani folk artists and the people or institutions seeking to book them. The idea comes from a visible gap: organisers, concert producers, cultural festivals, universities, tourism bodies, wedding and event planners, diaspora organisations and international cultural programmes often ask for Rajasthani folk performers, while many talented artists do not have a professional channel through which to present their work. At the same time, artists and groups approach RLA seeking help in finding performances and reaching larger audiences.
RLA will respond by creating a structured, five-level artist documentation and promotion system. First, the team will research and categorise artists. Second, it will create a consolidated internal repository. Third, it will develop detailed and verified profiles. Fourth, it will publish artist stories and portfolios on RLA’s social media channels. Fifth, it will publish approved profiles on a dedicated website through which organisers can browse artists and submit booking enquiries.
RLA will act as a connector and booking facilitator: understand an organiser’s event, location, date, art-form preference, duration, technical needs and budget; shortlist suitable artists; contact them; coordinate introductions and proposals; and support the booking process. The artist and organiser will confirm availability, performance terms, travel, accommodation, payment, cancellation and other conditions in writing. RLA should communicate transparently about any facilitation fee or commission before a booking is confirmed.
The project will begin with in-person visits and interviews in Western Rajasthan, where several important folk traditions and artist networks are concentrated, including Manganiyar and Kalbelia performers. After testing the process there, RLA will expand to other regions of Rajasthan in a phased manner.

2. Why Are We Doing This?
The problem we are trying to solve
Organisers frequently need folk artists for concerts, festivals, cultural evenings, university events, tourism programmes, conferences, weddings, public celebrations and international cultural showcases, but they may not know whom to contact.
Many highly skilled artists have limited online presence, no standard professional profile, few quality recordings, or no direct access to large event organisers and cultural institutions.
Artist information is scattered across personal contacts, local networks, social media posts and informal referrals. Organisers cannot easily compare art forms, group size, experience, repertoire, location, technical requirements or indicative budget.
Artists themselves often approach organisations and individuals asking for help to find shows, reach new audiences and connect with organisers.
Potential opportunities can be lost because an organiser needs a quick, reliable shortlist, while artists need help presenting their work professionally and responding to enquiries.
Some artists perform individually, while others work in family ensembles, troupes or larger groups. Organisers need clear information about each format.
The opportunity for DVP
DVP can use its youth network, community relationships and digital reach to create a trusted artist discovery and booking-facilitation platform. The project is not only an archive or cultural directory. It is a practical bridge between talent and demand, with cultural documentation at its foundation and artist visibility and opportunity as its central purpose.

4. The Five-Level Artist Documentation System
Every artist will move through a staged process. The five levels make the work manageable, prevent the team from trying to create polished promotional content for every lead immediately, and help RLA distinguish raw research from verified, bookable profiles.


5. What Each Artist Profile Should Contain
Basic identity and category
Artist ID; preferred public name; individual, duo, family ensemble, troupe or larger group.
Home district and area; exact village or personal location published only with permission.
Primary and secondary art forms, including relevant regional tradition or style.
Languages/dialects, instruments, repertoire and specialisation.
Solo or group performance; number of performers and available performance formats.
Expertise and experience
Years of practice or professional performance, where the artist can confirm it.
How the artist learned the tradition and any teachers or lineage details they choose to share.
Types of programmes performed: concert, festival, cultural evening, workshop, demonstration, educational event or private function.
Selected past performances, institutional appearances, awards or media coverage, with evidence where available.
Distinctive strengths: vocal style, instrument expertise, dance form, storytelling, repertoire or regional specialisation.
Booking and production information
Preferred event types and audience size, where relevant.
Typical performance duration and possible set formats.
Group size, instruments, sound requirements and stage/space needs.
Travel availability within Rajasthan, elsewhere in India and internationally, where applicable.
Indicative fee range or quotation process, if voluntarily provided; distinguish artist fee from travel, accommodation, equipment, taxes and other costs.
Public booking contact or representative, only with explicit permission.
Media and permissions
Artist-approved photographs, video clips, audio samples and existing public links.
Separate permission for social media use, website publication and sharing with organisers.
Credits for the artist, photographer, videographer or other rights holders.
Date of last verification and a process to request corrections or profile removal.
6. Consolidated Repository: One Source of Truth
RLA should maintain one master repository from which internal reports, social media content and website profiles are prepared. The team should not keep separate unlinked lists for each channel, as this leads to duplicate records and outdated information.


Access should be role-based. Private phone numbers, personal addresses and internal fee/negotiation notes must not be exposed through public exports or website pages.
7. Booking Facilitation: How RLA Will Bridge Artists and Organisers
The booking system should be designed around the organiser’s requirements, not just around a list of artists. An organiser may know they want a Rajasthani folk performance but may not know the names of traditions, the size of group they need or the production requirements. RLA will help clarify those requirements and present suitable options.

Booking principles
RLA acts as a bridge and facilitator; it must not promise an artist, date or price before confirmation from the artist or authorised representative.
An enquiry is not a confirmed booking. Confirmation requires written agreement and any agreed deposit or paperwork.
Show organisers a suitable range of options when possible, including emerging and less widely known artists.
Explain all costs transparently: artist fee, travel, accommodation, local transport, sound/lighting, taxes and RLA facilitation fee or commission, if any.
Set out how RLA earns revenue before launch. Possible models include a clearly disclosed booking facilitation fee paid by the organiser, a disclosed commission agreed with the artist, or a project-funded free facilitation service. Do not apply hidden charges.
For international engagements, verify passport/visa/work permissions, contracts, payment/currency terms, insurance, travel, equipment and any applicable legal or tax requirements with qualified advisers.
Do not publish an artist’s private phone number, fee negotiation or availability notes without permission.
8. Website and Social Media Strategy
A. Social media: make the artists visible
After Level 3 verification and artist approval, RLA can publish consistent features such as:
Meet the Artist: short biography, home region, art form and journey.
Know the Tradition: explain a music, dance or storytelling form in accessible language.
Artist at Work: short approved performance clip or instrument demonstration.
Solo Artist / Folk Ensemble: make clear whether the booking is for one performer or a group.
Behind the Voice: artist-approved account of training, repertoire and cultural context.
Available for Enquiries: an opt-in call to action directing organisers to the website or project contact, without suggesting guaranteed availability.
B. Website: help organisers discover and enquire
Search and filters by art form, district/region, instrument, language, solo/group format and performance type.
Artist profile pages with biography, expertise, experience, selected performances, group size and approved media.
A clear “Enquire about booking” button or form that sends the request to the RLA project team.
An organiser enquiry form capturing event date, location, budget, performance duration, group size and technical needs.
A privacy notice, booking process, terms of facilitation, media/copyright policy and correction/removal contact.
An internal admin panel or secure workflow to manage enquiries, contact artists and record outcomes.

9. Step-by-Step Regional Fieldwork Plan
The project will start with in-person travel and interviews in Western Rajasthan. This first phase is important because it allows the team to meet artists, understand their needs directly, build trust, test the profile form and learn how artist groups organise their performances. Manganiyar musicians, Kalbelia performers and other traditions in the region are an initial focus, not the only eligible artists.

The order is indicative and can be adjusted based on seasonal events, artist availability, travel costs, local introductions and safety. Western Rajasthan should be the first learning phase, followed by other regions rather than attempting a state-wide launch without testing the method.
10. Field Interview Process
Before travel: contact artists or local intermediaries, explain the project, agree on a time and place, and clarify that participation is voluntary.
At the interview: introduce RLA and the project; ask permission before taking photographs, audio or video; use the artist’s preferred language where possible.
Document the artist’s own account: how they learned the tradition, what they perform, what makes their practice distinctive, and what kind of opportunities they want.
For groups: interview a representative and, where possible, identify the group’s authorised contact, member count, available configurations and decision-making process.
Record practical booking details only where the artist is comfortable sharing them; confirm that indicative fees are not a final quote.
After the interview: enter the information into the repository, verify names and spellings, label all media, record permissions and share the draft profile for approval.
Follow up: send the artist their approved profile or link and explain how to request updates or removal.

16. Ethics, Rights and Safeguards
Obtain informed consent before collecting personal information or recording interviews.
Take separate permissions for the internal repository, social media, website publication and sharing media with prospective organisers.
Allow artists to review and correct profiles; provide a clear route to request updates or removal.
Do not promise bookings, income, government recognition or international travel simply because an artist is listed.
Obtain permission from the appropriate rights holder before publishing or forwarding recordings, photographs or performances.
Credit artists and media creators; do not edit recordings in ways that misrepresent a performance or cultural context.
Keep private contact details, non-public fee information and negotiation notes restricted to authorised staff.
Use respectful community terminology and consult tradition bearers when describing practices or lineages.
For minors, apply appropriate guardian consent, age-appropriate assent and stricter safeguards for media publication.
For international engagements, encourage written contracts and verify travel, immigration/work permissions, insurance, tax and payment requirements with qualified advisers.

20. Proposed Public Description

Many talented folk artists have extraordinary skills and generations of cultural knowledge but lack a professional channel to reach concert organisers, festivals, institutions and other presenters. At the same time, organisers often seek Rajasthani folk performances but do not have one reliable place to discover artists, understand their expertise or request a suitable group.
Desert Voice will build a consolidated, verified artist repository; develop professional profiles; share artist-approved stories and performances on social media; and publish a searchable website with a booking enquiry facility. RLA will help match organiser requirements and budgets with suitable artists, coordinate communication and support the booking process transparently.
Beginning with in-person interviews in Western Rajasthan and expanding across the state, the project aims to make folk artists more visible, discoverable and connected to opportunities while respecting their consent, cultural knowledge and rights.
Conclusion
Desert Voice should be implemented as an artist discovery, documentation, promotion and booking-facilitation initiative—not only as a cultural archive. Its distinguishing value is the connection it creates between two groups that often struggle to find each other: talented Rajasthani folk artists seeking opportunities and organisers seeking suitable performers.
The recommended sequence is to build and test the five-level workflow in Western Rajasthan, consolidate the data in one repository, publish the first approved profiles, launch a simple website enquiry process, and then expand region by region. The project should measure both the quality of documentation and the real opportunities it helps facilitate.
