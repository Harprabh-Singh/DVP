Roots & Wisdom

We are connected not just by geography, but by the words of wisdom passed down through generations.

Hover over the map

Marwari Kahavate

"पधारो म्हारे देस"

(Padharo Mhare Des)

Meaning: "Welcome to my land." The ultimate symbol of Rajasthani hospitality, inviting everyone with an open heart.

"ऊँट रे मुँह में जीरौ"

(Oont re munh mein jeero)

Meaning: "A cumin seed in a camel's mouth." Used when a resource provided is too small for a very large need.

"अक्कल बड़ी के भैंस?"

(Akkal Badi ke Bhains?)

Meaning: "Is wisdom bigger or the buffalo?" Emphasizing that intelligence and logic are always superior to brute strength.

Our Story

Founded in 2023 by a small group of students in Delhi, the Rajasthan Leaders Association was born from a simple idea: to create a "home away from home" for everyone arriving from Rajasthan.

We know the challenges of moving to a new city—finding a place to live, navigating a new campus, and building a professional network. RLA was created to be the support system we all wished we had.

Today, we are a thriving community of hundreds of students and professionals across India's major cities, united by our shared heritage and a passion for helping each other succeed.

Our Mission

To unite, support, and empower students and professionals from Rajasthan in their new cities, fostering a strong community through cultural, social, and professional engagement.

Our Vision

To be the most impactful and trusted support network for the Rajasthani diaspora, creating a legacy of leadership, service, and cultural pride across India and the globe.

Girls' safety in delhi : Take the Blinders off !!

By Vaidhansi Choudhary| Oct 28, 2025

Girls' safety in delhi : Take the Blinders off Delhi is the heart of India, full of hustle-bustle, dazzling historic monuments and night that never seems to rest. Yet for many women and girls, the city's shine carries a persistent doubt: how safe are they really? Through recent crime rates, police data and real time experiences we can say that daytime is usually hassle-free, but evenings are isolated and raise anxiety levels. Let's unfold the facts, highlight high risk zones and shield you with practical tips so you can live better in Delhi without constantly being watchful and uneasy. According to NCRB(National Crime Records Bureau) 2023 report indicate Delhi as the city with highest number of crimes against women among India 19 metros, logging roughly 14,158 cases. Within this total, 1204 rapes, 129 dowry deaths and thousands of molestation and harassment cases were recorded. This was the data of only those cases recorded. Delhi police have identified 17 police-stations zones that consistently register higher incidents of crime against women. These include those areas that often combine dense populations, poorly lit streets and limited police presence after dark including Dwarka district, Central delhi, Govindpuri, Mayur Vihar, etc. Government authorities are constantly committed to bring a changes to these vulnerabilities for that they have launched many plans and initiatives such as: * Special Women & Child units and Crime Against Women cells operate now in every district, headed by an ACP., Nirbhaya Funds projects have funded one-stop crises centres, night-patrol vans, and Himmat Plus safety app., Partnerships with NGOs, Resident Welfare Associations and educational institutions aiming to create community watch groups. With this practical safety measures are what we should be aware of i.e, while travelling stick to metro or trusted cab services (OLA/UBER with verified drivers, prebook women-only cab and women's couch in metro, avoid isolated areas and bus stops after 8pm. Keep phone fully charged + powerbank is must, install safety apps (Himmat plus, my safecity), share live location with friends, geo-tracking, instant SOS, walk in well lit, crowded lane, Dress comfortably, travel in groups, use buddy system for college or office trips. Choose hostels/hotels with 24 hour security and women-only floors. Memorise police helpline and no of nearest police stations. Carry safety tools, pepper spray etc. Besides these let us talk about major myths and what are the realities. It is believed that delhi is completely unsafe for women but the reality is many central and south delhi localities stay lively and safe till late, Risk spikes in isolated pockets, not across whole city. Women-only cabs are a hype but they provide an extra layer of comfort, especially during late-night travel, as drivers are verified and service is traceable. At last I would conclude by saying that 'Empowerment through Awareness' is what can keep delhi's charm charming for women. Stay informed about high-risk areas, choosing reliable transport and moving in groups when possible. Even though authorities are adding CCTV, better lighting and dedicated units, but personal vigilance remains the strongest shield. The best mantra is strong self, don't be anxious be cautious.

Education System in Delhi. Expections VS Reality

By Kishmat Takhar| Oct 28, 2025

Delhi University (DU) is one of India's leading universities, renowned for its academic excellence and cultural vibrancy. It attracts students from across India and diverse parts of the world, all of whom bring varied expectations about the educational experience they envision. These students often anticipate a blend of rigorous academics, world-class infrastructure, vibrant campus life, and ample opportunities for holistic growth. However, the reality they encounter once they join DU colleges can sometimes be a mix of fulfilling experiences and unforeseen challenges. This explores the expectations vs reality for students enrolling in DU colleges, highlighting their academic journey, infrastructural situation, and campus life. Expectations of Students Joining DU Colleges Students coming to DU from different regions expect a high academic standard given the university's national prestige and international reputation. The university, with over 90 affiliated colleges and an enrollment exceeding hundreds of thousands of students, is seen as a gateway to promising futures. Many expect a curriculum that is not only rigorous but also flexible and interdisciplinary, allowing them to explore diverse fields. They anticipate modern facilities such as well-equipped libraries, spacious classrooms, advanced laboratories, and digital resources. Faculty quality is another major expectation. Students hope to learn from experienced professors who act as mentors and provide guidance beyond textbooks. Alongside academics, students look forward to a campus enriched with cultural activities, sports, seminars, and clubs that foster social interaction and personal growth. The inclusive environment created by mixing students from all over the country and abroad adds to the appeal, promising a rich cultural exchange. Reality Faced by Students in DU Colleges The ground reality for many students varies depending on the specific college and course. Premier colleges like St. Stephen's, Hindu College, Lady Shri Ram College, and Shri Ram College of Commerce consistently meet or surpass expectations by offering robust academic programs, experienced faculty, and vibrant extracurricular activities. These colleges boast reasonably good infrastructure with libraries, lecture halls, and student amenities that support a conducive learning environment. However, several affiliated colleges face infrastructural challenges. Many colleges suffer from outdated buildings, overcrowded classrooms, and limited access to digital learning tools. Despite increased funding and government grants in recent years—such as the Rs 1,912 crore infrastructure overhaul announced in 2025 to build new campuses, academic blocks, hostels, and cultural centers—improvements are still underway and uneven across the university spectrum. Another area of concern is the teaching methodology. While DU is gradually implementing changes inspired by India's National Education Policy 2020 to transition from traditional exam-centric approaches to more research and application-oriented learning, many students continue to experience a system heavily focused on rote memorization and written tests. This shift is more palpable in reputed colleges and elite departments but less so in smaller or less-resourced institutions. Admission into popular courses and colleges is competitive, with merit-based selection being strict, which sometimes poses adjustment challenges to students from boards with different evaluation standards. Despite these pressure points, most students appreciate DU's culturally diverse environment, which allows them to grow socially and understand multiple perspectives. Infrastructure Developments and Challenges Delhi University is currently undergoing one of its biggest infrastructural transformations in decades. Over Rs 1,900 crore has been allocated towards several ambitious projects including a new college at Fatehpur Behri, extensive solar panel installations, enhanced surveillance systems for safety, and the construction of new academic blocks and hostels. For instance, the Institution of Eminence Hostel, a gender-inclusive residential complex capable of accommodating over 1,400 students, is nearing completion. New law academic blocks and computer centers are also being built. These developments represent a move towards a safer, more modern university environment. However, some colleges still operate with makeshift classrooms using portacabins and struggle with overcrowding due to limited space and heritage building constraints that prevent expansion. Safety audits have been mandated across DU colleges following various incidents involving infrastructure mishaps, underscoring the need for continuous maintenance and modernization. Student Experiences and Reviews The student community at DU offers a mixed but insightful perspective. Many praise the university's vibrant student life, the intellectual rigor of courses in premier colleges, and the active student unions and societies organizing cultural festivals and academic conferences. They find value in the diverse peer groups and the opportunities to develop leadership and soft skills. On the other hand, students often critique the uneven quality of infrastructure and faculty, overemphasis on theoretical exams, and the need for greater practical learning and research opportunities. While metropolitan colleges may offer notable placement support and internships, this is less consistent campus-wide. Some students also mention bureaucratic hurdles in administration and support services. Conclusion Delhi University colleges continue to be a sought-after destination for students aspiring for higher education in India. The expectations they bring—ranging from world-class education and infrastructure to a vibrant cultural environment—are partially met by many DU colleges with a strong tradition of excellence and ongoing reforms. Nonetheless, a substantial part of the university still grapples with infrastructure shortcomings, teaching method constraints, and resource disparities. Continuous investment, such as the Rs 1,900 crore infrastructure push and policy reforms, aim to bridge these gaps and provide a more equitable and modern educational experience. Understanding this nuanced reality empowers prospective students to set realistic expectations, adapt effectively, and make the most of their time at Delhi University.

How to Stay Rooted in Rajasthani Tradition

By Niharika Dular | Oct 28, 2025

How to Stay Rooted in Rajasthani Tradition While Living in an Urban City Like Delhi Moving from the golden sands of Rajasthan to the fast-paced lanes of Delhi is like stepping from the rhythm of folk music into the beat of city life. Delhi, with its skyscrapers, cafés, metro rush, and cosmopolitan vibe, makes it easy to lose touch with the simplicity and grace of our Rajasthani roots. Yet, somewhere between the chaos of Connaught Place and the calm of a Rajasthani home-cooked thali, there lies a beautiful possibility to carry the desert with you, even in the city. I've always believed that being Rajasthani is not just about where you live; it's about how you live. It's in the values, the colors, the food, the songs, and the way we treat people. Living in Delhi taught me that keeping my culture alive doesn't mean resisting change; it means blending tradition with modernity in a way that feels natural and personal. The Language of Belonging The first thing I noticed when I came to Delhi was how language changes the way people connect. While Hindi dominates conversations here, there's a different warmth in saying "Ram Ram sa" instead of a simple hello. I make it a point to use small Rajasthani phrases like "Khamma Ghani sa," etc. not just with Rajasthani friends but even with Delhiites curious to learn. It feels like carrying a piece of home in my words. Language is an identity. When I speak in my dialect, I'm not just communicating; I'm remembering my nani's stories, the village fairs, and the sound of folk songs echoing through desert nights. Even in an urban crowd, these little phrases remind me where I belong. Dressing the Culture, Not Just the Trend Delhi fashion is ever-evolving from streetwear to minimalistic western looks but there's something timeless about Rajasthani attire. I've learned to blend both worlds. Sometimes it's a cotton kurta with a colorful bandhej dupatta, or oxidized jewelry inspired by Jodhpur's markets. These aren't just fashion choices; they're cultural statements. I don't wear leheriya saree daily, but when I do, maybe during college events or festive days, I see how it turns heads. It's not just the color; it's the confidence that comes from wearing your culture proudly. Urban spaces might change your wardrobe, but they don't have to erase your roots. Instead, they can amplify them. The Taste of Home in a City of Cafes No matter how many cafes and food joints Delhi offers, nothing matches the comfort of dal-baati-choorma, gatte ki sabzi, or ker-sangri. Cooking traditional food in a city kitchen is my way of staying connected. Sometimes, I invite friends over for a "Rajasthani thali night." They come curious and leave amazed at how something so simple can taste so rich. Through food, I share stories of my land of how baati was once the warrior's meal and how choorma adds sweetness to the spice of life. Even when I eat out, I love exploring Rajasthani restaurants across Delhi. Places like Rajdhani Thali or Rajasthan Food Corner in Karol Bagh make me feel like I've stepped back home for a while. Food, after all, is the easiest way to stay rooted. The Spirit of Folk in Modern Celebrations Festivals in Delhi feel different. They're louder, more modern, and often celebrated in smaller spaces compared to the open courtyards back home. Still, I've found my own way to keep a bit of Rajasthani touch alive in them. During Diwali, I light mitti ke diye instead of fancy lights and put on soft folk songs while decorating my room. Even a simple bandhej dupatta used as a table cover makes me feel closer to home. I don't always get the time or space to dance to folk tunes or celebrate like we do back in Rajasthan, but the spirit remains. Sometimes it's just a small thing such as wearing leheriya bangles on Teej or sharing homemade ghewar with friends. These gestures might be small, but they carry that same festive warmth. I've realized that you don't need a crowd to celebrate your culture. Sometimes, a few meaningful acts are enough to keep traditions alive in a city apartment. Values That Outshine the City Lights In Rajasthan, we grow up with deep-rooted values such as respect for elders, hospitality, and a sense of community. In Delhi, where life moves fast and individualism often dominates, I try to carry forward that spirit of mehmaannawazi. When someone visits my room, they'll always find a cup of tea waiting maybe with some mathri or bhujia. These small gestures might seem ordinary, but they're the essence of who we are. Rajasthani culture is built on warmth, not wealth; on kindness, not competition. Living in a city like Delhi, these values become my quiet rebellion against indifference. Finding the Rajasthani Tribe When I first came to Delhi, I honestly didn't expect to meet many people from Rajasthan. But slowly, through college and student circles, I started noticing familiar surnames, accents, and the occasional "Khamma Ghani." That's how I found my small Rajasthani circle here. We don't meet every weekend or have grand get-togethers, but whenever we do, there's an instant comfort, a shared understanding that doesn't need words. Whether it's talking about mirchi vada cravings, missing family festivals, or just laughing about how Delhi winters hit differently, that connection feels like home. And even beyond that, I've realized that you don't need a big "tribe" to feel rooted. Sometimes, just one or two people who share your background are enough to remind you that you're not alone in keeping your culture alive. Art, Music, and the Desert Soul For me, art and music are more personal than public. I might not sing folk songs on stage or paint mandanas, but I do love listening to Rajasthani songs on quiet evenings especially when I'm homesick or missing the warmth of familiar places. A song like Kesariya Balam can instantly make me feel calmer, like I'm back under the evening sky of my hometown. Sometimes, I scroll through old photos of Jaisalmer, Udaipur, or Pushkar just to soak in the colors and feel that connection again. Even watching short videos of Rajasthani fairs or local artists online makes me proud. It's like my culture is right there, just a click away. Living in Delhi has taught me that even if you're not actively "doing" something artistic, simply appreciating your culture, its music, colors, and rhythm is enough to keep the desert alive within you. Modern, Yet Rooted There's a misconception that being traditional means being old-fashioned. But I see tradition as something living. Something that evolves with us. I can wear jeans with a bandhej scarf, eat pasta with chaach, or post Rajasthani proverbs as Instagram captions. That's the beauty of being a modern Rajasthani. I don't have to choose between my heritage and my present. I can be both. Delhi has taught me independence, confidence, and adaptability. Rajasthan has given me humility, color, and depth. Together, they make me who I am which is a beautiful blend of desert grace and urban spark. Conclusion: The Desert Within the City Being Rajasthani in Delhi is not about resisting change; it's about remembering who you are amid change. It's about carrying the desert's calm in a city's chaos. It's about keeping your roots strong while letting your branches grow. Every time I walk through Delhi's streets, I carry a part of Rajasthan in my heart, in my words, clothes, food or actions. Because no matter where life takes me, I'll always be that girl who says "Ram Ram sa," wears leheriya on special occasions, and never forgets that home is not just a place instead it's a way of living.

Balancing Academics, Fests, Societies, and Personal Life:

By Mahesh Puri | Oct 28, 2025

Balancing Academics, Fests, Societies, and Personal Life: The Real DU Challenge By Mahesh Goswami “College isn’t about doing everything — it’s about doing what matters, without losing yourself in the process.” Campus Life at Full Speed At Delhi University, every day feels like a rush. Classes in the morning, society meetings in the afternoon, fest rehearsals in the evening — and somewhere in between, you try to find time for yourself. It’s exciting, but also exhausting. DU life teaches you early on that balance doesn’t just happen; you have to build it. 1. Knowing What Matters First The biggest DU dilemma isn’t workload — it’s choice. There’s always something happening: a debate, a dance practice, a new event to volunteer for. The trick is to stop trying to do it all. Start by asking, What really matters to me this semester? Maybe it’s improving your grades, leading a team, or simply enjoying your time on campus. When you know your priorities, you can focus without guilt and still enjoy what you do. 2. Planning That Actually Works Not everyone needs a color-coded planner to stay organized. A simple weekly outline — on your phone or notebook — can make a huge difference. Keep track of deadlines, rehearsals, and free days. Try to keep one day light each week — your “reset day.” That bit of flexibility helps when plans inevitably change. The goal isn’t strict scheduling; it’s staying aware of your time instead of being ruled by it. > “A rough plan beats no plan — it keeps you grounded when everything feels chaotic.” 3. Staying Consistent with Studies It’s easy to let academics slide when college life is buzzing with activity. But consistency beats cramming every single time. Attend key lectures, revise briefly every few days, and don’t depend entirely on “notes from friends.” You don’t have to be the topper to be responsible. Studying steadily — even in small doses — keeps stress manageable when exam season hits. 4. Choosing Societies That Fit You Societies are DU’s soul. They build creativity, teamwork, and confidence — but overcommitment can drain your energy fast. Instead of joining five and missing meetings in all of them, pick one or two that genuinely inspire you. Whether it’s theatre, music, debating, or photography, give your best there. Contributing deeply to one community feels far more rewarding than showing up half-heartedly to many. 5. Handling Fest Season Without Burning Out Fest season is the heartbeat of DU — bright lights, sleepless nights, and endless energy. Everyone wants to be everywhere, but that’s when balance really matters. Take breaks, eat well, and don’t skip rest just because “everyone’s still awake.” Missing one event won’t make you less involved, but exhaustion can make you miss everything that comes next. 6. Protecting Personal Time Your personal time is just as important as your college time. Spend it how you want — not how others expect you to. Go for a walk at Ridge Road, sit quietly in the college lawns, or just enjoy a cup of chai with friends after class. College life is full of noise; your quiet moments will keep you sane. 7. Learning to Say “No” Saying yes to everything feels right at first — until it doesn’t. Learn to say “no” without guilt. Not every event, collaboration, or plan is meant for you. Respect your limits, and others will too. Boundaries don’t make you less social; they make you sustainable. 8. Accepting Imperfect Balance No one in DU has it perfectly sorted. Some weeks, you’ll ace your classes. Other weeks, you’ll barely touch your notes because fest rehearsals took over. And that’s okay. Balance isn’t a final state — it’s a rhythm you keep adjusting. What matters is self-awareness and the willingness to realign when things slip off track. > “Some imbalance is part of growth — what counts is how you return to balance.” Conclusion: The DU Way of Living Delhi University is more than lectures and certificates — it’s where you learn how to handle life in motion. Balancing academics, societies, and personal time isn’t about perfection. It’s about learning to manage chaos, prioritize meaningfully, and stay true to yourself amid it all. When you look back, you won’t remember how many things you did — you’ll remember how deeply you lived them. That’s the real DU balance: thoughtful, messy, and beautifully human.
